# Liftly — Frozen Beginner Programs

This document defines the fixed beginner hypertrophy programs used by Liftly V1.

**Revision 1.1 (2026-10-08, approved by Rohit):** swap-only exercises given full metadata (section 3a); Assisted Pull-up replaced by Machine Pulldown; Overhead Triceps Extension named Overhead Cable Triceps Extension; Upper 2 Seated Cable Row swap changed to One-Arm Dumbbell Row and Lower 1 Leg Extension swap changed to Goblet Squat, so no swap repeats an exercise in the same workout; 45-Degree Back Extension progresses by external load (section 6). See DECISIONS.md, P1 to P4.

These programs are designed for users with approximately **0–12 months of training experience** whose primary goal is **muscle hypertrophy** in a standard commercial gym.

The programs are intended to work with **Progression Engine v1.0**. Exercise order, working sets, rep ranges, exercise class, equipment type, and approved swaps should be treated as reference data for V1.

---

## 1. Programming Principles

Starting beginners with approximately **9–12 weekly sets per major muscle group** is an evidence-based and practical starting approach.

Higher weekly training volumes can increase hypertrophy, but returns diminish as volume increases. Beginners can make substantial progress at lower volumes than trained lifters, while lower starting volume may reduce excessive soreness and fatigue and leave room for future increases.

Free weights and machines can both support hypertrophy. Liftly uses a mixture of machines and dumbbells:

- **Machines** provide stability and reduce balance and coordination demands.
- **Dumbbells** provide simple free-weight exposure and develop basic motor control.
- Highly technical barbell lifts are not required for Liftly V1.

### Standard Rest Times

| Exercise class | Rest |
|---|---|
| Compound | 2–3 minutes |
| Isolation | 1.5–2 minutes |

# 4. Weekly Set Targets

The programs are designed around approximately **9–12 weekly sets for major muscle groups**, with smaller muscle groups receiving direct work plus secondary stimulus from compound exercises.

Approximate weekly direct-set targets from the research draft:

| Muscle group | Approximate weekly sets |
|---|---:|
| Chest | 9 |
| Back | 9 |
| Quads | 9 |
| Hamstrings / Glutes | 9 |
| Shoulders (anterior / lateral) | 6 direct sets plus pressing stimulus |
| Biceps / Triceps | 2–3 direct sets plus pulling / pressing stimulus |
| Calves | 3–6 |

These values are starting targets for V1, not advanced bodybuilding volume prescriptions.

# 5. Exercise Selection Rationale

## Machines and Dumbbells

Liftly prioritizes machines and dumbbells because both can support hypertrophy while keeping exercise selection practical for beginners.

Machines reduce balance requirements and provide more external stability. Dumbbells provide simple free-weight exposure and help develop basic motor control.

## Barbell Back Squat and Conventional Barbell Deadlift

These movements are intentionally excluded from Liftly V1.

The research draft identifies them as more technically demanding and more likely to be limited by coordination, lower-back fatigue, or technique rather than by the target muscle. This makes them less suitable for a beginner app that relies on consistent rep and effort logging.

Liftly instead uses exercises such as:

- Leg Press
- Goblet Squat
- Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

## Approved Swaps

Every V1 exercise has exactly one approved swap.

The swap should preserve the general movement pattern or target-muscle stimulus while providing an alternative when the original exercise cannot be used.

Examples:

- Machine Chest Press → Dumbbell Bench Press
- Seated Cable Row → Chest-Supported Row
- Leg Press → Goblet Squat

Approved swaps are fixed reference data. Claude does not choose exercises or invent swaps.

# 6. Progression Engine Compatibility

These programs must follow the existing **Progression Engine v1.0** rules.

### Compound exercises

- Rep range: **8–12**
- Progression uses the exercise's real gym load ladder.

### Isolation exercises

- Rep range: **10–15**
- Progression uses the exercise's real gym load ladder.

### Bodyweight exercises with external load

- 45-Degree Back Extension starts at bodyweight. Its ladder's first step is 0 kg added load, and the next steps are the extra loads the user's gym actually has (for example plates or dumbbells held).
- Progression moves along that ladder like any other exercise. Loads are recorded as added load in kg.

### Program sequence

Workouts operate as an ordered sequence:

- 3-day: `A → B → C → A → ...`
- 4-day: `U1 → L1 → U2 → L2 → U1 → ...`

The sequence does not reset because a weekday was missed.

# 7. V1 Boundaries

These programs apply only to:

- Beginner users with approximately 0–12 months of training
- Muscle-building / hypertrophy goals
- Standard commercial gyms
- Kilogram-based equipment
- Liftly V1

Not included:

- Home-workout programming
- Powerlifting or strength-specific programming
- Advanced bodybuilding periodization
- User-created routines
- Claude-generated exercise programs

Changes to these programs require a numbered Liftly specification revision.

# 8. Research Basis

The research supplied for these programs referenced the following areas:

- Resistance-training volume and diminishing returns
- Beginner volume requirements
- Machine versus free-weight hypertrophy outcomes
- Compound versus isolation exercise selection
- Beginner-friendly exercise selection

Sources cited in the supplied research draft included:

- Schoenfeld et al. — dose-response research and meta-regression work on resistance-training volume and hypertrophy.
- Research comparing machine-based and free-weight resistance training.
- ACSM-style resistance-training guidance supporting combinations of multi-joint and single-joint exercises.
